⚡ Free Access Must Always Be Preserved

If a company includes Pythos in a commercial product, users must still have a clear, direct, functional path to the official free version of Pythos.

https://pythos.lanzar.me/

That path must not require any of the following:

✦ What Commercial Use Is Permitted

Commercial organizations are welcome to build products and services around Pythos. The license permits a wide range of commercial activity.

A company may charge for its own product while including Pythos — provided the link to the official free Pythos service is clearly, directly, and prominently available to users without payment.

"Our $20/month platform includes Pythos." — permitted, provided users are also clearly told: "Pythos is available free at pythos.lanzar.me" — and that link genuinely works without payment.

PFEL v2.0 §5.1

⊘ What Is Not Permitted

The license prohibits arrangements that eliminate or obstruct free access to Pythos — regardless of how those arrangements are labeled or structured.

✕ Prohibited

Making payment a prerequisite for accessing Pythos itself.

✕ Prohibited

Hiding the free Pythos link in legal terms, footer footnotes, or inaccessible locations.

✕ Prohibited

Placing the free link behind authentication — only accessible after logging into the commercial product.

✕ Prohibited

Routing users through a payment or subscription gate before they can follow the free link.

✕ Prohibited

Stating or implying that payment is required to access Pythos, without clarifying the free alternative.

✕ Prohibited

Claiming there is no free version of Pythos.

✕ Prohibited

Technically blocking or interfering with user access to the official free Pythos service.

✕ Prohibited

Deliberately crippling a Covered Work solely to make a paid product appear superior by comparison.

⚖️ The Distinction

The license draws a clear line between two different situations:

✓ Permitted

Paying for a product that includes Pythos — with the free access path clearly preserved and the official free link prominently available to users.

✕ Prohibited

Paying for access to Pythos itself — where Pythos functionality is the sole or dominant purpose of the charge, and providing a free link does not cure the violation.

See PFEL v2.0 §5 for the complete definition of this distinction and the objective tests that apply.

📋 License Status & Source Availability

PFEL v2.0 is not an OSI-approved Open Source license.

The OSI Open Source Definition requires that licenses not restrict commercial use. PFEL v2.0's Free Access Preservation requirement does restrict certain commercial arrangements, which means it falls outside the OSI definition.

         only inside an authenticated or paid product interface,
         it does not satisfy this requirement unless it also
         appears in a pre-authentication, pre-purchase location
         satisfying (d)(i) or (d)(ii).

         discourage Users from noticing or following it.

         (i)  must be rendered in a font size no smaller than

         (ii) must be rendered with a text-to-background color

     - Paying for a product that INCLUDES Pythos, with free

     - Paying for ACCESS TO PYTHOS ITSELF → prohibited, even
